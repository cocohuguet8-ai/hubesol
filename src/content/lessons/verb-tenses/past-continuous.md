## How to form the past continuous

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + was/were + verb-**ing** | She **was working**. / We **were waiting**. |
| Negative | subject + wasn’t/weren’t + verb-ing | They **weren’t listening**. |
| Question | Was/Were + subject + verb-ing? | **Were** you **sleeping**? |

Use **was** with *I, he, she, it* and **were** with *you, we, they*. In a question with a question word, was/were comes next: *What **were** you **doing**?*

**Short answers:** *Yes, I was. / No, they weren’t.*

**Time expressions:** *at eight o’clock last night, this time yesterday, all evening, while, when.*

## Spelling of the -ing form

| Rule | Verb ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| add -ing | most verbs | walk → walking, play → playing |
| drop -e, add -ing | verbs ending in *-e* | make → making, write → writing |
| double the consonant | short verb, consonant–vowel–consonant | run → running, stop → stopping |
| -ie → -ying | verbs ending in *-ie* | lie → lying, die → dying |

Verbs ending in *-w*, *-x* or *-y* do not double the last letter (*snowing, fixing, playing*).

## When to use the past continuous

### 1. An action in progress at a past time

The action started before that moment and was still going on.

- At ten o’clock last night, **I was studying.**
- This time yesterday, **we were flying** to Rome.

### 2. A longer action interrupted by a shorter one

The long action is the past continuous. The short action that interrupts it is the past simple.

- **I was having** a shower **when** the doorbell **rang**.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~I had a shower when the doorbell rang.~~ → ✓ **I was having a shower when the doorbell rang.** (The longer action was already in progress.)

### 3. Two actions at the same time

Often with *while*. Both actions are past continuous.

- **While** she **was cooking**, he **was setting** the table.

## When and while

| Word | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| while | before the longer action in progress | I fell **while** I **was skiing**. |
| when | before the short action that interrupts (past simple) | I was skiing **when** I **fell**. |
| at + time | start or end of the sentence | **At eight last night**, I was cooking. |
| all evening / all day | end of the sentence | They were playing games **all evening**. |

## State verbs

Some verbs describe a state, not an action in progress: *know, want, like, love, understand, believe*. They use the past simple, not the continuous.

- ✗ ~~I was knowing the answer.~~ → ✓ **I knew the answer.**

## Common mistakes

**1. Past simple for the action in progress.** If the action was already going on when another action happened, use the past continuous. ✗ *~~I had a shower when the doorbell rang.~~* → ✓ **I was having a shower when the doorbell rang.**

**2. Wrong was/were.** ✗ *~~You was sleeping.~~* → ✓ **You were sleeping.** Was = I/he/she/it; were = you/we/they.

**3. Past continuous with a state verb.** ✗ *~~I was wanting a new bike.~~* → ✓ **I wanted a new bike.**

**Pro tip:** ask yourself, “Was the action already going on at that moment?” If you can picture it in progress, use was/were + -ing.
