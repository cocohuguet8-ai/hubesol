## The difference at a glance

| | Past Simple | Past Perfect |
|---|---|---|
| When | the main past event | before another past event |
| Order | the later action | the earlier action |
| Form | past form (*worked, went*) | had + past participle (*had worked, had gone*) |
| Example | I **got** home. | Everyone **had left**. |

The key question: which action came **first**? The earlier action takes the past perfect; the later action takes the past simple.

## How to form them

| Form | Past Simple | Past Perfect |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + past form | subject + had + past participle |
| | She **left** at six. | She **had left**. |
| Negative | subject + didn’t + base verb | subject + hadn’t + past participle |
| | He **didn’t call**. | He **hadn’t called**. |
| Question | Did + subject + base verb? | Had + subject + past participle? |
| | **Did** you **eat**? | **Had** you **eaten**? |

Watch the irregular verbs: *eat – ate – eaten, go – went – gone, see – saw – seen*. More in [Irregular Verbs](/grammar/verb-tenses/irregular-verbs/).

## When to use the past simple

### 1. The main events, in order

When you tell events in the order they happened, the past simple is enough.

- **I woke up, got dressed and left.**

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~I had woken up, had got dressed and had left.~~ → ✓ **I woke up, got dressed and left.**

### 2. Finished past time

- We **moved** here in 2015.

## When to use the past perfect

### 1. The earlier of two past actions

Use it to show that one action happened before the other.

- When I **woke up**, the sun **had** already **risen**.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~When I got to the station, the train left.~~ → ✓ **When I got to the station, the train had left.** (It left first.)

### 2. The reason for a past situation

- She **passed** the exam because she **had studied** hard.

### 3. Experience up to a past moment

- I **had never seen** snow before I moved to Canada.

## When, by the time, before, after

| Word | What it shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| when + past perfect | the action was already finished | **When** I arrived, they **had eaten**. |
| when + past simple | one action right after the other | **When** I arrived, they **ate**. |
| by the time | the earlier action was complete at that moment | **By the time** we got there, the shop **had closed**. |
| before / after | the order is already clear, so both tenses are possible | **After** she **(had) finished**, she went home. |

## Signal words

| Word | Usually goes with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| already / just | past perfect | The film **had already** started. |
| never … before | past perfect | I**’d never** flown **before**. |
| yesterday / last week / ago | past simple | We **met** two years **ago**. |
| then / after that | past simple | I ate, **then** I went out. |

## Common mistakes

**1. Past simple for both actions when the order matters.** ✗ *~~By the time we arrived, the film started.~~* → ✓ **By the time we arrived, the film had started.**

**2. Past perfect for a simple sequence.** ✗ *~~She had opened the door and had walked in.~~* → ✓ **She opened the door and walked in.**

**3. Wrong participle after had.** ✗ *~~They had ate.~~* → ✓ **They had eaten.**

**Pro tip:** draw a timeline. If one action is clearly further back in the past than the story, give it the past perfect; everything else stays in the past simple.
