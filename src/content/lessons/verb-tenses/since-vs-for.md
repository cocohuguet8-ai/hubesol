## How to choose

| Word | Goes with | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **since** | a point in time (when it started) | since 9am, since 2019, since Monday, since I was a child |
| **for** | a length of time (how long) | for ten minutes, for three days, for years, for a long time |

The quick test: can you count it (*two hours, three weeks, ten years*)? Then it’s **for**. Does it name **the moment something started** (*Monday, 2019, lunchtime*)? Then it’s **since**.

**Questions:** to ask about the length of time, use **How long…?** *How long have you lived here? — For five years. / Since 2020.*

## The tense: present perfect

*Since* and *for* usually go with the **present perfect** (have/has + past participle) or the **present perfect continuous** (have/has been + verb-ing), because the situation started in the past and continues now.

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present perfect | have/has + past participle + since/for | I**’ve known** her **since** 2015. |
| Present perfect continuous | have/has been + verb-ing + since/for | She**’s been working** here **for** six months. |
| Negative | haven’t/hasn’t + past participle + since/for | We **haven’t seen** him **since** June. |

## When to use since and for

### 1. Since + a starting point

*Since* tells you **when** the situation began. The point can be a day, a date, a time or a past event.

- I’ve known her **since** 2015.
- He’s been ill **since** Monday.
- Everything has changed **since** we moved.

### 2. For + a period of time

*For* tells you **how long** the situation has lasted.

- We’ve lived here **for** five years.
- They’ve been waiting **for** an hour.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~I’ve lived here since five years.~~ → ✓ **I’ve lived here for five years.** (*Five years* is a length of time.)

### 3. For in other tenses

*For* also works with finished periods in the past simple and with plans in the future. *Since* almost always needs a perfect tense.

- I lived in London **for** two years. (I don’t live there now.)
- We’re going to Italy **for** a week.

## Since + a clause

After *since* you can use a full clause with the **past simple**: the clause names the moment it started.

- I’ve lived here **since I was** a child.
- She hasn’t called **since she left** the company.

✗ ~~I’ve lived here since I have been a child.~~ → ✓ **I’ve lived here since I was a child.**

## Signal words

| Word | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| since + point | end of the sentence (or the start) | I’ve been here **since nine o’clock**. |
| for + period | end of the sentence | She’s worked here **for three years**. |
| how long | start of the question | **How long** have you known him? |
| ever since | end of the sentence | We met in 2010 and we’ve been friends **ever since**. |
| all my life | end of the sentence (no *for* needed) | I’ve lived here **all my life**. |

## Common mistakes

**1. Swapping since and for.** Check whether it is a point or a period. ✗ *~~for 2019~~* → ✓ **since 2019**, and ✗ *~~since three days~~* → ✓ **for three days.**

**2. Present simple for a situation that continues now.** In English, a situation that started in the past and continues now needs the present perfect. ✗ *~~I live here for five years.~~* → ✓ **I’ve lived here for five years.**

**3. Present perfect in the since-clause.** The clause after *since* names a finished moment, so it uses the past simple. ✗ *~~since we have met~~* → ✓ **since we met.**

**Pro tip:** put a number in front of it. If *two*, *three* or *ten* fits (*two days, ten years*), use **for**; if it doesn’t (*~~two Monday~~*), use **since**.
