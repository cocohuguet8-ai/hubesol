## How to form the future continuous

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + will be + verb-**ing** | She**’ll be working**. |
| Negative | subject + won’t be + verb-ing | They **won’t be coming**. |
| Question | Will + subject + be + verb-ing? | **Will** you **be joining** us? |

**Will be** is the same for every subject, and the main verb always takes **-ing**. The verb *be* is never left out. In a question with a question word, *will* comes next: *What **will** you **be doing** at ten?*

**Short answers:** *Will you be working on Saturday? — Yes, I will. / No, I won’t.*

**Time expressions:** *this time tomorrow, this time next week, at 8pm tonight, all day tomorrow, in an hour, when you arrive.*

## Spelling of the -ing form

| Rule | Verb ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| add -ing | most verbs | watch → watching, play → playing |
| drop -e, add -ing | verbs ending in *-e* | drive → driving, use → using |
| double the consonant | short verb, consonant–vowel–consonant | sit → sitting, swim → swimming |

## When to use the future continuous

### 1. An action in progress at a future time

The action will start before that moment and still be going on.

- At 8pm tonight, **I’ll be watching** the match.
- This time next week, **we’ll be relaxing** on the beach.

### 2. Polite questions about plans

It sounds softer than *will* + base verb, because you are asking about plans, not making a request.

- **Will** you **be using** the printer this afternoon?
- **Will** you **be needing** anything else?

### 3. A longer action interrupted by a future event

- **I’ll be cooking** when you arrive, so come straight to the kitchen.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~At nine tomorrow I’ll work.~~ → ✓ **At nine tomorrow I’ll be working.** (The action will be in progress at that moment.)

## Future continuous or future perfect?

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| At ten, I**’ll be writing** the report. | in progress at ten |
| By ten, I**’ll have written** the report. | finished before ten |

More in [Future Perfect](/grammar/verb-tenses/future-perfect/).

## State verbs

*Know, want, like, believe, own* describe states, so they don’t use the continuous. Use *will* + base verb.

- ✗ ~~I’ll be knowing the answer soon.~~ → ✓ **I’ll know the answer soon.**

## Common mistakes

**1. Continuous for a completed future action.** ✗ *~~By ten I’ll be finishing the report.~~* → ✓ **By ten I’ll have finished the report.**

**2. Leaving out be.** ✗ *~~I will working at nine.~~* → ✓ **I’ll be working at nine.**

**3. Continuous with a state verb.** ✗ *~~She’ll be wanting a coffee.~~* → ✓ **She’ll want a coffee.**

**Pro tip:** imagine a photo of that future moment. If the action is happening in the photo, use *will be* + -ing.
