## How to form the future perfect

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + will have (**’ll have**) + past participle | She**’ll have left**. |
| Negative | subject + won’t have + past participle | They **won’t have finished**. |
| Question | Will + subject + have + past participle? | **Will** you **have arrived** by then? |

**Will have** is the same for every subject — never *will has*. In a question with a question word, *will* comes next: *How many books **will** you **have read** by June?*

**Short answers:** *Will you have finished by six? — Yes, I will. / No, I won’t.*

**Time expressions:** *by Friday, by then, by 2030, by the time…, before…, in two years’ time, by the end of the month.*

## The past participle

Regular verbs add **-ed** (*finish → finished*). Irregular verbs use the third form.

| Base | Past participle | Base | Past participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| go | gone | write | written |
| see | seen | take | taken |
| do | done | eat | eaten |
| leave | left | build | built |

More in [Irregular Verbs](/grammar/verb-tenses/irregular-verbs/).

## When to use the future perfect

### 1. Completed by a future point

The action will be finished before a certain time in the future. Often with *by*.

- By Friday, **I’ll have finished** the report.
- **She’ll have graduated** by next summer.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~By 2030 I finish my studies.~~ → ✓ **By 2030 I’ll have finished my studies.**

### 2. With by the time

*By the time* + present simple, then the future perfect for the earlier action.

- **By the time** you **read** this, **I’ll have gone**.

### 3. Predictions about the past (it’s probably done)

- Don’t call now — they**’ll have gone** to bed.

## By and by the time

| Word | Followed by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| by | a time or date | **By six**, we’ll have eaten. |
| by the time | a clause in the present simple | **By the time** she **arrives**, we’ll have eaten. |
| in … time | a period | **In two years’ time**, I’ll have saved enough. |

✗ ~~By the time she will arrive~~ → ✓ **By the time she arrives** (no *will* after *by the time*).

## Future perfect or future continuous?

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| By ten, I**’ll have finished**. | done before ten |
| At ten, I**’ll be finishing**. | in progress at ten |

## Common mistakes

**1. Present simple for a completed future action.** ✗ *~~By June I finish the course.~~* → ✓ **By June I’ll have finished the course.**

**2. Wrong participle after will have.** ✗ *~~will have went~~* → ✓ **will have gone.**

**3. Will after by the time.** ✗ *~~By the time you will get here, we’ll have left.~~* → ✓ **By the time you get here, we’ll have left.**

**Pro tip:** future perfect = done by then; future continuous = in progress then.
