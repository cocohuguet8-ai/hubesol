## How to form the future with will

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + will (**’ll**) + base verb | She **will call** later. / I**’ll help**. |
| Negative | subject + won’t + base verb | They **won’t come**. |
| Question | Will + subject + base verb? | **Will** you **help** me? |

**Will** is the same for every subject — it never adds *-s* and is never followed by *to*. In speech we usually contract it to **’ll** (*I’ll, she’ll, we’ll*), and the negative is **won’t** (= will not). In a question with a question word, *will* comes next: *When **will** you **know**?*

**Short answers:** *Will it be hot? — Yes, it will. / No, it won’t.*

**Time expressions:** *tomorrow, tonight, next week, next year, soon, in two days, in 2050, one day.*

## Will with I think, probably and maybe

Predictions often come with words that show how sure you are.

| Word | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I think / I don’t think | start of the sentence | **I think** it’ll rain. / **I don’t think** she’ll come. |
| probably / definitely | after *will*, before *won’t* | He’**ll probably** be late. / He **probably won’t** come. |
| maybe / perhaps | start of the sentence | **Maybe** we’**ll** win. |
| I’m sure | start of the sentence | **I’m sure** you’**ll** pass. |

## When to use will

### 1. Instant decisions

A decision made at the moment of speaking.

- The phone’s ringing. — Don’t worry, **I’ll get** it.
- **I’ll have** the salad, please.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~I get it.~~ (deciding right now) → ✓ **I’ll get it.**

### 2. Predictions and opinions

What you think or believe about the future.

- I think it **will rain** tomorrow.
- **You’ll love** this film.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ✗ ~~It rains tomorrow.~~ → ✓ **It will rain tomorrow.** (A prediction takes *will*.)

### 3. Promises, offers and requests

- I promise **I’ll call** you tonight.
- **Shall** I open the window? / **Will** you **open** the window, please?

## Shall for offers and suggestions

With *I* and *we*, use **Shall** in questions to offer help or make a suggestion. Don’t use *Will I…?* for this.

- **Shall I** carry your bag? (an offer)
- **Shall we** go to the cinema? (a suggestion)

✗ ~~Will I open the window?~~ → ✓ **Shall I open the window?**

## Will or going to?

| Situation | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| deciding now | will | I’m thirsty. — **I’ll get** you a drink. |
| a plan made before now | going to | **I’m going to visit** my aunt on Sunday. |
| prediction from what you can see now | going to | Look at the sky! **It’s going to rain.** |
| opinion about the future | will | **I think** you**’ll** like her. |

More in [Will vs Going to](/grammar/verb-tenses/will-vs-going-to/).

## Common mistakes

**1. Will for plans you already made.** ✗ *~~I will meet Sara tomorrow.~~* (already arranged) → ✓ **I’m meeting Sara tomorrow.**

**2. Changing the form of will.** ✗ *~~She wills come.~~ / ~~She will to come.~~* → ✓ **She will come.**

**3. Present simple for predictions.** ✗ *~~In 2050, cars drive themselves.~~* → ✓ **In 2050, cars will drive themselves.**

**Pro tip:** one form fits every subject — *will* + base verb. If you decide as you speak, it’s *will*.
