## How to form can, could and be able to

| Form | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | can / can't + base verb | She **can drive**. I **can't swim**. |
| Past | could / couldn't + base verb | I **could read** at four. |
| Other forms | be able to + base verb | I'd like **to be able to** cook. |

*Can* and *could* are modal verbs: they never take **-s**, never take **to** after them, and make questions and negatives without *do*.

| ✗ | ✓ |
|---|---|
| ~~She cans swim.~~ | She **can** swim. |
| ~~I can to play.~~ | I **can play**. |
| ~~Do you can come?~~ | **Can** you come? |
| ~~I don't can dance.~~ | I **can't** dance. |

## When to use *can*

### 1. Present ability

- My brother **can speak** three languages.
- **Can** you **ride** a bike?

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~My sister can to play the piano.~~ → No *to* after *can*: *My sister can play the piano.*

### 2. Possibility and general truths

- You **can see** the sea from my window.
- It **can get** very cold here in winter.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~It cans be cold.~~ → Modals never take *-s*: *It can be cold.*

## When to use *could*

### 1. General ability in the past

Something you were able to do over a period of time.

- I **could swim** when I was five.
- My grandma **couldn't read** until she was twelve.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~When I was young, I can climb trees.~~ → Past ability takes *could*: *I could climb trees.*

### 2. Senses and understanding in the past

*Could* is very common with *see, hear, smell, understand, remember*.

- We **could hear** music from the flat upstairs.
- I **couldn't understand** the instructions.

## When to use *be able to*

### 1. Forms that *can* doesn't have

*Can* has no infinitive, no *-ing* form and no perfect form, so we use *be able to*.

| Need | Example |
|---|---|
| future | I**'ll be able to** drive next year. |
| present perfect | I **haven't been able to** sleep. |
| after *want / like / hope* | I'd love **to be able to** sing. |
| after another modal | You **might be able to** help me. |

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~I will can come tomorrow.~~ → Two modals can't go together: *I'll be able to come tomorrow.*

### 2. One success in the past

For **one specific** successful action in the past, use **was/were able to** or **managed to**, not *could*.

- The fire was big, but everyone **was able to** escape. (✓ — not ~~could escape~~)
- I **managed to** finish the exam just in time.

In the **negative**, *couldn't* is fine for one situation: I **couldn't** open the door.

## Questions and short answers

| Question | Short answers |
|---|---|
| **Can** you swim? | Yes, I **can**. / No, I **can't**. |
| **Could** she walk at one? | Yes, she **could**. / No, she **couldn't**. |
| **Will** you **be able to** come? | Yes, I **will**. / No, I **won't**. |

## Common mistakes

**1. Adding *to* after *can* or *could*.** *~~I can to swim~~* → **I can swim.**

**2. Adding *-s* in the third person.** *~~He cans~~* → **He can.**

**3. Two modals together.** *~~I will can~~* → **I will be able to.**

**4. *Could* for one past success.** *~~After three hours, we could find the hotel~~* → **we were able to find / we managed to find the hotel.**

**Pro tip:** if you need *can* in a tense it doesn't have, replace it with *be able to* and change *be*.
