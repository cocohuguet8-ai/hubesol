## How to ask for and give permission

| Modal | Formality | Asking | Giving / refusing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **can** | informal, everyday | **Can** I borrow your pen? | Yes, you **can**. / No, you **can't**. |
| **could** | more polite | **Could** I open the window? | Yes, of course. / Sure, go ahead. |
| **may** | formal, very polite | **May** I come in? | Yes, you **may**. / No, you **may not**. |

All three are modal verbs: **base verb** after them, no *-s*, no *to*, no *do* in questions.

## When to use *can*

### 1. Asking friends and family

- **Can** I use your phone?
- Mum, **can** I go to Laia's house?

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~Can I to go out?~~ → No *to* after *can*: *Can I go out?*

### 2. Rules and what is allowed

*Can* and *can't* describe rules: what is or isn't permitted.

- You **can** park here after 8 p.m.
- Students **can't** use their phones in class.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~You don't can smoke here.~~ → The negative is *can't*: *You can't smoke here.*

## When to use *could*

### 1. Polite requests for permission

*Could* is softer than *can*. Use it with teachers, strangers or when the favour is bigger.

- **Could** I leave ten minutes early today?
- **Could** we sit here?

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~Yes, you could.~~ (as an answer) → We don't give permission with *could*: *Yes, you can.* / *Yes, of course.*

### 2. Past permission

*Could* also means something was allowed in the past.

- When I was ten, I **could** stay up until nine.
- We **couldn't** wear trainers at my old school.

## When to use *may*

### 1. Formal requests

- **May** I ask a question?
- **May** I see your passport, please?

### 2. Formal permission and rules

*May* appears on signs, notices and in official language.

- Visitors **may** take photos without flash.
- Passengers **may not** smoke on the train.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~Visitors mayn't enter.~~ → Don't contract *may not*: *Visitors may not enter.*

## Polite answers

| Yes | No |
|---|---|
| Yes, of course. | I'm sorry, you can't. |
| Sure, go ahead. | I'm afraid not. |
| Yes, you can / may. | No, you may not. (formal) |
| No problem. | I'd rather you didn't. |

## Other ways to talk about permission

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| be allowed to | We**'re** not **allowed to** run in the corridor. |
| Do you mind if…? | **Do you mind if** I sit here? |
| let + person + verb | My parents **let** me **stay** out until ten. |

*Be allowed to* has all the tenses: I **won't be allowed to** go.

## Common mistakes

**1. *To* after the modal.** *~~May I to come in?~~* → **May I come in?**

**2. *Do* in the question.** *~~Do I can sit here?~~* → **Can I sit here?**

**3. Giving permission with *could*.** *~~Yes, you could.~~* → **Yes, you can.**

**4. *Let* with *to*.** *~~My mum lets me to go~~* → **My mum lets me go.**

**Pro tip:** the more important the person or the favour, the more polite the modal: *can* → *could* → *may*.
