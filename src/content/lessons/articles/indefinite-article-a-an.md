## What is the indefinite article?

**A** and **an** go before a **singular countable noun**. They mean "one" of something, but not a specific one we both know. (Choose *a* or *an* by the first **sound** of the next word — see below.)

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| A new or non-specific thing (a/an = "one") | I saw **a** dog in the park. I need **a** pen. Wait **a** minute. |
| Job or role | She's **a** nurse. |
| Per (every) | twice **a** week, 50 km **an** hour |
| Exclamation | What **a** day! |

## A or an? Choose by sound

Use **a** before a **consonant sound** and **an** before a **vowel sound**. It depends on how the word is *pronounced*, not on how it is spelled.

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| **an** + vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u sounds) | **an** apple, **an** egg, **an** umbrella |
| **a** + consonant sound | **a** book, **a** car, **a** house |
| Silent *h* → vowel sound → **an** | **an** hour, **an** honest man, **an** honour |
| *u* pronounced /juː/ (like "you") → consonant sound → **a** | **a** university, **a** uniform, **a** European city |
| Pronounced *h* → **a** | **a** hotel, **a** horse |

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~an university~~ → *a university* · ~~a hour~~ → *an hour*

## When to use a / an

### 1. A new or non-specific thing

When we mention something for the first time, or mean any one of many, the listener doesn't know which one yet.

- I bought **a** new jacket yesterday.
- There's **a** cat on the roof.
- I need **a** pen. — Can I have **a** coffee, please?
- Wait **a** minute. (A/an here means "one": *Wait one minute* is also correct, with more stress on the number.)
- Lisbon is **a** beautiful city. (one of many beautiful cities)

### 2. Jobs and roles

When you say what someone's job is, use *a/an* with the singular job word. (Plural jobs, like *They're doctors*, and titles used as a unique role, like *She became president*, don’t take *a/an*.)

- My mum is **a** teacher. — He wants to be **an** engineer.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~She is doctor.~~ → *She is a doctor.*

### 3. Per (every): time, speed, price

*A/an* can mean "per" or "every".

- I go swimming three times **a** week.
- The car was going at 100 kilometres **an** hour.
- Apples are two euros **a** kilo.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~twice the week~~ → *twice a week*

### 4. Exclamations with *what*

- What **a** great idea! — What **an** amazing view!
- With plurals and uncountable nouns there is no article: What lovely weather! — What terrible shoes! (not ~~What a lovely weather!~~)

## When NOT to use a / an

### 1. Plural nouns

- ~~a books~~ → **books** / **some books**

### 2. Uncountable nouns

- ~~a water~~, ~~an information~~, ~~an advice~~, ~~a news~~, ~~a homework~~ → **some water**, **some information**, **a piece of advice**, **some news**, **some homework**
- Other common uncountable nouns: *money, help, furniture, milk, weather*.
- Some drinks can be countable when we mean a portion: **a coffee** (= a cup of coffee).

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~Can you give me an advice?~~ → *Can you give me some advice?*

### 3. Before a possessive

- ~~a my friend~~ → **a friend of mine** / **my friend**

## Have / There is + a / an

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| have got + a | I've got **a** brother. |
| there is + a | There's **a** bank near here. |
| Is there + a | Is there **a** café? |

## Common mistakes

**1. No article with jobs.** *~~He's mechanic.~~* → **He's a mechanic.**

**2. A with plurals.** *~~I have a cats.~~* → **I have cats.**

**3. A with uncountables.** *~~a homework~~* → **some homework**.

**4. Missing article with singular nouns.** *~~I have car.~~* → **I have a car.**

**Pro tip:** a singular countable noun almost never stands alone — it needs **a/an**, **the**, or a word like **my**.
