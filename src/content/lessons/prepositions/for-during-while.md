## How to choose for, during or while

| Word | Followed by | Answers the question | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **for** | a length of time | How long? | I studied **for** two hours. |
| **during** | a noun (an event or period) | When? | I fell asleep **during** the film. |
| **while** | subject + verb (a clause) | When? | I fell asleep **while** I was watching the film. |

## When to use *for*

### 1. How long something lasts

- We waited **for** twenty minutes.
- She lived in London **for** three years.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~I studied during two hours.~~ → A length of time takes *for*: *for two hours.*

### 2. With the present perfect

- I've known her **for** ten years.

See [Since vs For](/grammar/verb-tenses/since-vs-for/).

## When to use *during*

### 1. At some point in a period or event

- My phone rang **during** the lesson.
- It rained a lot **during** the night.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~during I was eating~~ → *During* needs a noun. With a verb clause use *while*: *while I was eating.*

### 2. All through a period

- The shop is closed **during** August.
- **During** the summer, we go to the beach every day.

## When to use *while*

### 1. Two actions at the same time

- **While** I was cooking, my brother set the table.
- She listens to music **while** she studies.

> ⚠️ Typical error: ~~while the lesson~~ → *While* needs subject + verb. With a noun use *during*: *during the lesson.*

### 2. *While* + -ing

When both clauses have the same subject, you can use *while* + -ing.

- **While walking** home, I saw an accident. (= while I was walking)

## *During* or *for*?

| *during* | *for* |
|---|---|
| tells you **when** | tells you **how long** |
| I slept **during** the flight. | I slept **for** two hours. |
| **during** the holidays | **for** two weeks |

Both are possible together: *I slept **for** two hours **during** the flight.*

## Quick test

| Next word is… | Use |
|---|---|
| a number + time (*two hours, a week*) | **for** |
| a noun for an event (*the film, the night*) | **during** |
| subject + verb (*I was…, she slept*) | **while** |

## Common mistakes

**1. *During* + length of time.** *~~during three days~~* → **for three days.**

**2. *During* + clause.** *~~during I was sleeping~~* → **while I was sleeping.**

**3. *While* + noun.** *~~while the match~~* → **during the match.**

**4. *For* to say when.** *~~for the film~~* (meaning at some point) → **during the film.**

**Pro tip:** look at what comes next — a number (**for**), a noun (**during**), or a person doing something (**while**).
